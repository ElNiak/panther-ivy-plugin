## Executive summary

**Verdict.** The plugin implements an unusually mature multi-agent verifier-in-the-loop architecture that lines up well with the paper's prescriptive content. The adversarial 3-of-3 vote pattern (G0–G8), the numbered pattern catalog (`#100`–`#599`), the inline G4 false-SOUND catcher, the bounded fix-attempt loop (cap = 3), and the read-only verifier all map directly to paper recommendations. Five **critical workflow defects** undermine that architecture: four dispatched specialist agents do not exist on disk, the load-bearing journaling-contract injection hook is not implemented, the RAG tools are mis-wired to the wrong workflow, the orchestrator name diverges from every failure-recovery route, and the iron laws are advertised as "binding" but only one has an enforcement hook (which exits 0).

**Counts.** 24 findings — 5 critical, 13 warning, 6 info.

**Top 3 critical findings.**

1. **Four agents cited as dispatch targets do not exist on disk.** `spec-analyst`, `model-reviewer`, `traceability-agent`, `plugin-conventions-reviewer` — every `Agent(subagent_type="panther-ivy-plugin:<name>")` to those four fails at runtime. Cited from `build-ops` Phase 3 + Phase 5, `verify-ops` Phase 6, `meta-self-mod-ops` Step 3, and `agent-dispatch.md` (which even publishes their `<dispatch-context>` schema). Paper §5.1: *"DSVA architecture utilizes a strict division of labor: Deconstruct Agent, Synthesize Agent, Verify Agent, Analyze Agent."* The plugin's division of labor is intended but mostly broken.

2. **RAG tools are accessible only to the reviewer, not to the builder.** `ivy_rfc` and `ivy_extract_requirements` appear in `ivy-reviewer-agent.md`'s `tools:` list but not in `ivy-builder-agent.md`'s. The builder is the agent that authors Ivy spec from RFC text. Paper §6.1: *"RAG significantly reduces syntax hallucinations by providing the LLM with exact, verified structural templates ... incorporating phase-specific RAG contributes a massive +15.1% accuracy boost ... during the critical deconstruct and synthesize phases."* The plugin has the RAG infrastructure but mis-wires it to the analysis phase rather than the synthesis phase.

3. **`hooks/scripts/inject-journaling-contract.py` is referenced as a hard gate but does not exist.** `journaling-contract.md` §"Subagent thread" and §9 say the hook MUST exit code 2 if the contract file is unreadable, blocking the dispatch. The actual hook registered at `SubagentStart` is `test-subagent-inject.sh`. Paper §3.2: *"strict deterministic isolation. Monadic architectures are frequently employed to treat execution errors as a 'no result' rather than a system violation."* The plugin advertises a determinism gate it does not actually run.

**Highest-leverage next step.** The four missing agents and the missing inject hook are mechanical fixes and unblock everything else. They should ship before any of the deeper refinements below. After that, re-wire `ivy_rfc` + `ivy_extract_requirements` into the builder agent and add a `Phase 2 Blueprint — RAG retrieval` step to `build-ops`; that single change is the closest match to the paper's empirically validated "+15.1% phase-specific RAG" gain (§6.1).

## Pillar A — Iterative Predict-then-Verify

> Paper §3, §3.1, §3.2. *"The industry standard has shifted completely away from single-shot generation. The vanguard of formal methods now relies on a dynamic 'Predict-then-Verify' loop ... Provided the empirical success rate p of the LLM's generative step is strictly greater than zero, the system will eventually converge on a correct, verified specification with a probability of one. The expected number of iterations to success is given by E[τ] = (1 − p) / p."*

The plugin implements this loop with discipline. The findings below are about edges that don't yet match paper prescriptions.

### A.1 — Fix-attempt loop is bounded and journaled (info, **GOOD**)

The reconnaissance pass mistakenly flagged this loop as unbounded; on inspection the cap exists.

**Evidence.** `skills/verify-ops/SKILL.md:271-307` — Phase 7 `<HARD-GATE>`. Counter is journaled via `progress{kind: "fix_attempt", key: "<file>", attempt: <int>}`; cap fires at `count >= 3`; escalation menu is presented via `AskUserQuestion`; cap can be raised only via a journal-visible `decision{kind: "override_attempt_cap"}` event.

**Paper alignment.** Matches §3 Markov-chain framing: bounded retry budget prevents the long-tail divergence the paper acknowledges (*"the probability of an LLM entering an infinite loop of failures decreases exponentially"*) but never quantifies p. The cap is a sound conservative ceiling.

**Severity.** info — keep this design.

### A.2 — `p` is not measured (warning)

**Paper.** §3: *"E[τ] = (1 − p) / p."* The paper repeatedly leans on per-step success rate `p` as the load-bearing parameter; no plugin instrument tracks it.

**Evidence.** `progress{kind: "fix_attempt"}` records *that* an attempt occurred but not *the verdict* of that attempt. No journal event aggregates pass-rate per spec, per protocol, per layer.

**Rationale.** Without `p`, the cap of 3 is a guess. If p empirically runs at 0.4 for QUIC and 0.15 for BGP, the BGP cap should be larger and QUIC should cap earlier. Operating without p is fine in early days; not knowing your p number a year in is a sign the loop is not being instrumented.

**Fix (description-only).** Add a `progress{kind: "fix_outcome", key: <file>, attempt: <int>, verdict: "pass" | "fail"}` event after the post-fix `ivy_verify`. A small Python helper in `scripts/` can compute rolling p from the journal; surface via `ivy_observability` or a new MCP tool.

### A.3 — `ivy_verify` failure is structured but sometimes opaque (warning)

**Paper.** §3.1: *"highly detailed, machine-readable error messages."* §3.2: *"granular feedback that pinpoints the exact boundary condition or loop invariant."*

**Evidence.** `verify-ops/SKILL.md:300+` shows the agent operates on a counterexample trace. `verification-failures/SKILL.md` references `references/counterexample-walkthrough.md`. The pattern catalog is numbered (`#100`–`#599`) and gate-sliced.

**The gap.** When `ivy_verify` returns FAIL with no counterexample (e.g., type error, unresolved symbol, Z3 timeout), the verifier has to infer pattern membership manually. `verification-failures/SKILL.md:33` documents this in prose ("the failure is likely a type error, unresolved symbol, or Z3 timeout — load `methodology` instead") but no automated classifier maps the raw `ivy_verify` payload to a catalog `#NN`.

**Fix (description-only).** Add a small classifier in `ivy_verify`'s LSP-side wrapper that pre-tags failures with a candidate pattern range (`#100–149` for solver, `#200–249` for decidability, `#400–499` for verdict, etc.) before returning. The agent then walks the candidate slice, not the whole catalog.

### A.4 — No incremental / cached verification (warning)

**Paper.** §3.2: *"Highly desirable tools require extensions like cached or batch verification ... the SMT solver must not re-verify unchanged legacy state components."*

**Evidence.** `iron-laws.md` § STALENESS_RULE re-runs `ivy_verify` whenever any file in the include closure changed. The closure is granular at the file level, not the isolate or invariant level. There is no facility to cache the verdict of unchanged isolates and only re-prove the touched ones.

**Fix (description-only).** Out of scope for a Markdown fix — would require Ivy-toolchain changes (per-isolate verdict caching keyed on AST hash). Track as research note; the PANTHER team's MCP roadmap (per `MEMORY.md`) is a natural home.

### A.5 — Deterministic isolation is partial (warning)

**Paper.** §3.2: *"strict deterministic isolation. Monadic architectures are frequently employed to treat execution errors as a 'no result' rather than a system violation, ensuring that if an LLM proposes highly corrupted logic, the system continues to the next hypothesis without corrupting the global statistical state."*

**Evidence — what works.** The verifier agent has `forbidden_tools: ["Edit", "Write"]` (`agents/ivy-verifier-agent.md:17`) and the builder agent has `forbidden_tools: ["Bash"]` (`agents/ivy-builder-agent.md:18`). Workflow journal allows warm resume. The `pending_dispatch` / `workflow_resumed` pair is turn-boundary-safe. ABSTAIN is first-class (per `gate-verdicts.md`).

**Evidence — what doesn't work.** The journaling-contract injection hook (the determinism gate for sub-agent threads) is missing on disk (Phase 1 critical finding). The `_VALID_EVENT_TYPES` enumeration in `workflow_state.py` is a closed list with silent rejection — a bug introduced via a `kind:` typo is undetectable. Sequential-write assumption (`journaling-contract.md` §4.2) has no `fcntl` lock.

**Fix.** See E.1 (inject hook implementation) and the warning on `_VALID_EVENT_TYPES` validation in plugin-validator's report.

## Pillar B — Multi-Agent Decomposition & Adversarial Refinement

> Paper §5. *"DSVA architecture utilizes a strict division of labor: Deconstruct Agent, Synthesize Agent, Verify Agent, Analyze Agent ... Alethfeld deploys LLMs in inherently adversarial roles. An 'Adviser' agent evaluates proof strategies, a 'Prover' agent generates the hierarchical steps, and an independent 'Evaluator' agent is explicitly prompted to relentlessly search for logical gaps or unverified dependencies."*

The plugin's architecture matches DSVA + Alethfeld more closely than any other formal-methods harness I'd expect to see. The findings below are about the gap between the architecture as designed and the architecture as shipped.

### B.1 — Four dispatched agents do not exist on disk (**critical**)

**Paper.** §5.1 and §5.2 above.

**Evidence.** Direct file-system check (2026-04-30):

```
ABSENT: spec-analyst
ABSENT: model-reviewer
ABSENT: traceability-agent
ABSENT: plugin-conventions-reviewer
```

Dispatch sites:

- `skills/build-ops/SKILL.md:130` — Phase 3 dispatches `spec-analyst` on compile error.
- `skills/build-ops/SKILL.md:317` and `:329` — Phase 5 dispatches `model-reviewer` + `traceability-agent` in parallel.
- `skills/verify-ops/SKILL.md:246` — Phase 6 dispatches `model-reviewer` for structural-issue audit.
- `skills/verify-ops/SKILL.md:317,431` — failure-recovery contract names `model-reviewer` (Opus tier).
- `skills/meta-self-mod-ops/SKILL.md` — Step 3 dispatches `plugin-conventions-reviewer`.
- `.claude/rules/agent-dispatch.md:10` — even publishes `<dispatch-context>` schema for the missing trio.

**Rationale.** The DSVA framing assigns Synthesize (builder), Verify (verifier), Analyze (reviewer + spec-analyst), Deconstruct (model-reviewer + traceability-agent in the build Phase 5 quality gate). With four of the eight roles missing, the workflow falls back on single-agent monolithic execution at exactly the points the paper flags as needing decomposition.

**Fix (mechanical, recommended).** Create the four agent files. Two-step path:

1. Decide whether each missing agent is (a) a real specialist that should be implemented, or (b) a role that has already been merged into an existing agent (e.g., the verifier already does counterexample interpretation inline; `spec-analyst` may be redundant). Audit each call site.

2. For each agent that survives Step 1, scaffold the agent file from the existing `<dispatch-context>` schema in `agent-dispatch.md:13–43` and the failure-recovery clauses in the same file. Use `ivy-verifier-agent.md` as a template. Suggested model tier: Opus for `model-reviewer` (already documented), Sonnet for the others.


### B.2 — Adversarial 3-of-3 vote is implemented well (info, **GOOD**)

**Paper.** §5.2 (Alethfeld), §5.1 (DSVA Verify Agent).

**Evidence.** `agents/g-fidelity-critic.md`, `agents/g-plan-critic.md`, `agents/g-knowledge-critic.md`. Each emits `VERDICT_<value>(#0X, "<drift>", "<plan-task>")` plus `Reasoning:` and `Recommendation` (only on UNSOUND). The orchestrator aggregates 2-of-3 (`skills/ivy/references/parallel-dispatch.md`). Spot-check mandate via `CITATION_PASS / CITATION_FAIL / CITATION_ABSTAIN` (per `g-fidelity-critic.md:54-62`) is the closest implementation I've seen of the paper's "evaluator agent ... explicitly prompted to relentlessly search for logical gaps."

**Severity.** info — keep this design.

### B.3 — No back-translation (NL ← Ivy) critic exists (**critical**)

**Paper.** §5.1: *"Verify Agent ... takes the generated MTL formula, translates it back into English, and performs a semantic similarity check against the original requirement to detect logical drift."* DSVA attributes a substantial share of its +17.99 percentage-point gain to this loop.

**Evidence.** No back-translation agent exists in `agents/`. The closest analogue is the `ivy_extract_requirements` MCP tool (which goes RFC → YAML, not Ivy → NL). G2/G3/G4 critics check Ivy-side patterns; no critic checks "does this Ivy spec actually capture RFC §X.Y's normative intent in plain English?"

**Rationale.** The paper's empirical claim is unusually clean here — the 90.9% accuracy of DSVA vs. 72.91% of TR2MTL baseline is attributable in significant part to back-translation. The plugin already has the infrastructure (3-of-3 vote, RFC retrieval via `ivy_rfc`, structured output schema), so adding the critic is marginal.

**Fix (description-only).** Add `agents/g-translation-critic.md` (Sonnet tier) with `<dispatch-context>` fields `target_files`, `rfc_source`, `claimed_translation`. Body: critic reads the Ivy invariant + the cited RFC section, drafts an NL paraphrase of the Ivy invariant, compares against the RFC text, returns `VERDICT_SOUND/UNSOUND/ABSTAIN` per the existing critic schema. Wire as a new G7 gate fired post-G2 in `assess-modeling.py`. Catalog ID range: reserve `#600–649`.

### B.4 — Sub-translation interactivity exists in build-ops phases but lacks human-review checkpoints (warning)

**Paper.** §5.3: *"nl2spec utilizes few-shot prompting to instruct the LLM to generate a dictionary of sub-translations ... providing a web-based interface where human engineers can visually verify, edit, or delete individual logical components."*

**Evidence.** `build-ops/SKILL.md` has 6 phases: Scope → Blueprint → Write → Verify → Quality → Wrap-up. Each phase has a "Situation Briefing" gate, a "Reflection Gate" pattern, and several `AskUserQuestion` checkpoints. The sub-translation idea is implemented at phase granularity (one sub-translation per phase boundary), not per-component.

**The gap.** Per-layer is coarser than per-invariant or per-monitor. When G2 flags a `[GAP: #NN]` marker, the user sees the top-level summary, not a side-by-side diff of "this is the RFC clause | this is the proposed Ivy invariant | here's the disagreement." The plugin's Markdown rendering (per `output-style.md`) gives the agent a way to surface this, but no skill structures it.

**Fix (description-only).** Add a `references/inline-diff-template.md` to `build-ops/` that the agent fills in when emitting a `[GAP: #NN]` marker, showing RFC quote / current Ivy / proposed Ivy / divergence. The user can then approve / amend / reject one sub-translation at a time.

### B.5 — G0b plan-fidelity gate has a critic but no documented dispatch trigger (warning)

The reconnaissance flagged this as an "orphan gate." On full reading, `agents/g-fidelity-critic.md` is well-defined (calibrated abstention, citation contract, edge cases). What's missing is the *dispatch trigger*.

**Evidence.** `agents/g-fidelity-critic.md:1` — *"Fires on the first action after a plan-approved dispatch to confirm the action conforms to the approved plan."* `gate-verdicts.md` references G0b as "plan-fidelity" in its master gate enumeration. But:

- No hook fires `g-fidelity-critic` automatically. The orchestrator owns the dispatch (per `skills/ivy/SKILL.md:69` table).
- The orchestrator's `references/` directory does not contain a `g0b_dispatch.md` analogous to `g0_dispatch.md` (which fires post-`ExitPlanMode`).
- The detection signal — "first action after a plan-approved dispatch" — is not mechanised. There is no journal event marking "this is the first plan-execution action."

**Fix (mechanical).**

```diff
# In skills/ivy/SKILL.md, replace the G0b row:
-| G0b plan-fidelity | `g-fidelity-critic` | First action after a plan-approved dispatch |
+| G0b plan-fidelity | `g-fidelity-critic` | First Edit/Write/Bash invocation after the orchestrator emits `workflow_resumed` paired with a `plan_approved` event |
```

```diff
# Add hooks/scripts/dispatch-g0b.py — fires on PreToolUse for Edit/Write/Bash
# Reads journal, finds last workflow_resumed with paired plan_approved, checks
# whether g0b_dispatched event exists since that pair. If not, dispatches
# g-fidelity-critic ×3 and writes gate_dispatched event. Exit 0 either way.
```

Add a `gate_dispatched{gate: "g0b"}` event class to `_VALID_EVENT_TYPES`.

## Pillar C — Prompt & Context Engineering

> Paper §4. *"CoT prompting augmented with retrieval examples achieved a 58% success rate with GPT-4 ... vs. a mere 19% success rate with direct contextless prompts."* §4.1: *"Negative constraints ... paradoxically increase the likelihood of the model attending to the forbidden concept."*

### C.1 — CoT scaffolding via phase decomposition (info, **GOOD**)

**Paper.** §4.1.

**Evidence.** Every ops-skill is a multi-phase procedure (`build-ops` 6 phases, `verify-ops` 7 phases, `review-ops` 4 phases). Within each phase, `Situation Briefing` and `Reflection Gate` patterns force the agent to articulate state before acting. The orchestrator's "knowledge questions vs do-something tasks" branching (`skills/ivy/SKILL.md:88-101`) prevents single-shot collapse.

**Severity.** info — strongly aligned with paper §4.1.

### C.2 — Heavy negative-constraint phrasing across skills, agents, and rules (warning)

**Paper.** §4.1 (verbatim above).

**Evidence (regex `\b(do not|don'?t|never|avoid|forbidden|MUST NOT|SHALL NOT|prohibited)\b`, count of occurrences):**

| File | Negative-constraint count |
|---|---:|
| `skills/verify-ops/SKILL.md` | 16 |
| `skills/review-ops/SKILL.md` | 15 |
| `skills/triage-ops/SKILL.md` | 11 |
| `skills/build-ops/SKILL.md` | 8 |
| `skills/ivy/SKILL.md` | 5 |
| `.claude/rules/skill-conventions.md` | 8 |
| `.claude/rules/iron-laws.md` | 5 |
| `.claude/rules/gate-verdicts.md` | 5 |

The iron-law names themselves are negative: `NO_FIX_WITHOUT_VERIFY`, `NO_LAYER_WITHOUT_SCAFFOLD`, `NO_QUALITY_WITHOUT_COVERAGE`. The paper's claim is that "negative constraints paradoxically increase the likelihood of the model attending to the forbidden concept."

**Rationale.** A complete rewrite is impractical and would cost clarity. But the highest-traffic phrases (the iron-law names + the most-recurring `do not` clauses in `verify-ops` and `review-ops`) are the load-bearing ones. Re-phrasing those to positive form is cheap.

**Fix (mechanical, partial).** Reframe the four iron-law names without losing the rule:

```diff
# .claude/rules/iron-laws.md and skills/ivy/SKILL.md "Iron-law primer"
-| NO_FIX_WITHOUT_VERIFY | verify | No "verification passed" claim without a fresh `ivy_verify` / `ivy_compile` tool result this turn. |
+| GROUND_FIX_IN_VERIFY | verify | Ground every "verification passed" claim in a fresh `ivy_verify` / `ivy_compile` tool result this turn. |
```

## Considerations

**Pro.** The report ties every finding to either a paper quote or to direct on-disk evidence (file:line or grep result). Severity is calibrated: `critical` reserved for runtime-fatal defects, `warning` for paper-prescription violations the plugin partially follows, `info` for confirmations of strong design or low-priority future work. Mechanical fixes ship as diffs that can be reviewed in PR; judgment-heavy items ship as descriptions to avoid premature syntax commitments.

**Con.** Two limits to flag. (1) The audit did not open every `references/*.md` file under each skill — `references/layer-scaffolding.md`, `references/counterexample-walkthrough.md`, `references/iut-output-analysis.md` may already address findings C.6, A.3, and parts of D.3 respectively. A follow-up pass should spot-check those before implementing the description-only items. (2) The paper's quantitative claims (CoT 58% vs 19%, RAG +15.1%, synergy +7.6 pp) come from benchmarks (DafnyComp, MTL, MBPP-Dafny) that don't directly match Ivy's domain. The directions are sound; absolute magnitudes for Ivy-on-protocols may differ. Treat the empirical numbers as priors, not predictions.

**Alternatives considered.** (1) A pillar structure aligned with the standard four-pillar harness audit (Context / Harness / Evals / Prompting) was rejected because the user explicitly asked for paper-rubric framing. (2) A description-only report (no diffs) was rejected because the most load-bearing fixes are mechanical and shipping diffs reduces the round-trip cost to apply them. (3) Including the eval-coverage analysis as a primary pillar was rejected because eval gaps are a derivative of the architectural issues already covered under E (the missing agents have no evals because they don't exist; the absent inject hook has no test because the implementation is absent). Eval coverage will become measurable after the critical fixes land.
